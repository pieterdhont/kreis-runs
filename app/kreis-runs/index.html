The runner

One sculpted body on a 19-bone skeleton, moved by physics across a full range of speeds. From about 0.8 m/s he walks: both feet touch the ground together for a moment and his body rises over each step. Around 1.6 m/s he blends into a jog and then a run. Above 3.2 m/s he moves into a sprint, reaching a full sprint at 10 m/s: up to about 5.4 steps a second, ground contact under a tenth of a second, landings on the ball of the foot with the heel never touching, high heel recovery, big knee drive and a wide, powerful arm swing. Each gait's step rate and ground-contact time come from measured human data scaled to his size. Planted feet never slide: each foot lands on the heel, rolls flat and pushes off from the toes, landing more on the forefoot and pushing off harder the faster he goes. He leans into acceleration and back when braking, each landing sends a small jolt up his body, and his pelvis turns and dips against his shoulders with every stride. His torso follows his limbs: the pelvis turns with his thighs and the chest with his arms, in true opposition. He sways over the loaded leg, his spine flexes on each landing and extends through the push-off, his shoulders move forward and back with each arm, and his breathing deepens with effort while his head stays steady.

His look

A Japanese-meets-Bauhaus hero, cel-shaded with ink outlines. He has a flame-coloured crest of hair and a wrap top with a family crest. A black obi with streaming tails and red-lined sleeves with ribbons complete the outfit, along with pleated hakama and a cape split into two tails. He blinks, and his eyes narrow, brows set and mouth opens as he runs faster. The cape, obi tails and sleeve ribbons are simulated cloth: pinned to his body, pulled by gravity and pushed by the air streaming past at his running speed. They drape at a walk, ripple in his wake at a run and flap out straight at a full sprint, and they bump against his back, sun disc, arms and legs instead of passing through.

The camera

Every angle can circle a full 360° around Kreis: turn on 360° circle, or drag the scene to orbit and tilt by hand. Zoom with the scroll wheel, a pinch, the slider or + −. Director cuts between seven shots, each with its own zoom move. Hold the move pad to slide the camera left, right, up, down, forward or back, along each axis separately. Reset view returns to the starting position.

Shortcuts

Space pause, 1–6 cameras, C 360° circle, arrow keys orbit and tilt, W A S D move the camera forward, left, back and right, Q E down and up, N day or dusk, Z dolly zoom, 0 reset zoom.
